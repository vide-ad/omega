01 One tap logs the set

There is no tick on a working set. You adjust the weight and reps if they are wrong, then tap how many you had left, and that tap does both jobs. This is your ruling from 11 September, and the reason it matters is that both numbers are already filled in for you, so a normal set costs you exactly one tap. Fewer than Strong, and effort comes free with it.

Warmups and AMRAP sets keep a plain tick, because effort means nothing on a warmup and an AMRAP set is zero by definition. Toggle either one and watch the chips swap for a button.

02 Brass means the engine is talking

The big number is a suggestion, never a record. Nothing else on the screen is brass, so an instruction never looks like something you did. Green is the opposite: a set you have actually banked. Violet marks anything that limits what the engine is allowed to conclude, so the chin-up waiting on your physio and the capped curl both carry it.

Red and pink belong to pain and to nothing else in the whole app.

03 The curl shows a limit, not a weight

Open the Incline Dumbbell Curl. Your ruling was that the app stays out of the decision near an injured nerve, so it prescribes nothing and shows you the physio's limits instead. You pick the weight. Notice it still counts your sets and still gives you a rest timer, because you are still training, it just is not telling you what to lift.

04 One exercise open at a time

The version I built before ran to about 6,600 pixels, roughly eight phone screens, because every exercise was expanded at once. Here the open one is the only one with detail and the rest are a name and a row of dots. Finish an exercise and the next one opens by itself.
